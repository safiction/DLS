INFORMATION RETRIEVAL ASSIGNMENT - CONCLUSIONS
==============================================

Dataset: BEIR nfcorpus/test
- 3,633 documents
- 323 queries with relevance judgments
- Graded relevance: 0-2


1. RANKER COMPARISON
--------------------

Four rankers were evaluated on nDCG@10, MAP, and MRR:

Ranker          nDCG@10    MAP        MRR
Boolean-OR      0.1429     0.0864     0.2266
TF-IDF          0.1932     0.1007     0.3391
BM25            0.2913     0.1392     0.5025
RRF Fusion      0.2402     0.1174     0.4112

Key findings:
- BM25 significantly outperforms all other rankers
- BM25 achieves 51% better nDCG@10 than TF-IDF (0.2913 vs 0.1932)
- RRF Fusion improves over TF-IDF but not over BM25 alone
- Boolean-OR baseline shows the value of proper ranking algorithms


2. BM25 PARAMETER SWEEP
-----------------------

Parameters tested:
- k1 (saturation): {0.5, 1.0, 1.2, 1.5, 2.0, 3.0}
- b (length normalization): {0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0}

k1 results (b=0.75):
k1=0.5:  0.2870
k1=1.0:  0.2901
k1=1.2:  0.2905
k1=1.5:  0.2913
k1=2.0:  0.2919  (best in sweep)
k1=3.0:  0.2904

b results (k1=1.5):
b=0:     0.2871
b=0.25:  0.2930  (best overall)
b=0.5:   0.2909
b=0.75:  0.2913
b=1.0:   0.2882

Best parameters found: k1=1.5, b=0.25 with nDCG@10=0.2930

Interpretation:
- k1 controls term frequency saturation; moderate values (1.5-2.0) work best
- b controls length normalization; partial normalization (b=0.25) is optimal
- Extreme values (b=0 or b=1) hurt performance


3. TOKENIZATION STUDY
---------------------

Three tokenization pipelines compared:

Tokenizer    Vocab Size  nDCG@10    MAP        MRR
basic        35,840      0.2913     0.1392     0.5025
stopwords    35,816      0.2914     0.1384     0.5040
stemming     31,251      0.3086     0.1443     0.5203

Key findings:
- Stemming provides the best performance (6% improvement over basic)
- Stopword removal has minimal impact (vocabulary reduced by only 24 terms)
- Stemming reduces vocabulary by 13% (4,589 terms) through conflation
- Conflation helps by matching morphological variants (e.g., "treatment" and "treatments")


4. STATISTICAL SIGNIFICANCE
---------------------------

Comparing BM25 vs TF-IDF on nDCG@10:
- Mean difference: 0.0981
- 95% CI: [0.0789, 0.1172]

Paired t-test:
- t-statistic: 10.03
- p-value: < 0.0001
- Significant at alpha=0.05: YES

Wilcoxon signed-rank test:
- statistic: 1685.5
- p-value: < 0.0001
- Significant at alpha=0.05: YES

Conclusion: The improvement of BM25 over TF-IDF is statistically significant with high confidence (n=323 queries).


5. OVERALL CONCLUSIONS
----------------------

BM25 vs TF-IDF:
BM25's term frequency saturation and document length normalization provide substantial improvements over TF-IDF. The saturation effect (k1) prevents long documents from dominating due to high term frequency, while length normalization (b) adjusts for document length bias.

Parameter Sensitivity:
BM25 is relatively robust to parameter choices around the default (k1=1.5, b=0.75), but tuning can provide small gains. The best found parameters (k1=1.5, b=0.25) suggest that less aggressive length normalization works better for this corpus.

Tokenization Impact:
Stemming is the most effective preprocessing step, improving all metrics by conflating morphological variants. Stopword removal has minimal effect, likely because common words in medical queries carry meaning.

Fusion:
RRF Fusion of BM25 and TF-IDF performs between the two individual rankers, suggesting that the rankers are correlated and simple fusion does not provide complementary benefits in this case.

Statistical Rigor:
The improvements observed are statistically significant (p < 0.0001), confirming that BM25's advantages are not due to chance.
